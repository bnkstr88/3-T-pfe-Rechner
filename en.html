Inflation and taxes

The returns above apply at 2% inflation. With higher inflation, bonds and the money market rise fully with it on average, equities by three quarters; with lower inflation they fall accordingly.

Taxes are calculated as in a real German account, with fixed assumptions:
  • flat-rate capital gains tax (Abgeltungsteuer) plus solidarity surcharge, together 26.375% on gains; no church tax
  • partial exemption (Teilfreistellung): 30% of gains from the equity ETF are tax-free, none for bonds and money market
  • saver's allowance of €1,000 a year
  • advance lump sum (Vorabpauschale) every year, with the 2026 base rate of 3.2%; it rises and falls with inflation
  • at the start, 30% of Pot 3 is already unrealised gain
Tax is due on every sale, including when the pots are refilled, and is paid from Pot 1. Losses are offset against later gains.

    How to split the money at the start

    Pot 1 · Money market ETF
    Pot 2 · Bonds
    Pot 3 · Global equity ETF

    Pot 1 pays the withdrawals for the next 12 months. Pot 2 is the reserve for crisis years, about 60% government and 40% investment-grade corporate bonds. Pot 3 grows over the long term. The pots are rebalanced once a year.

    How is it calculated? And what does research say?

    Process: Every year, Pot 1 is topped up again. If equities are no more than 10% below their peak, the money comes from Pot 3, and Pot 2 is topped up too. If they are lower, it comes from Pot 2 and the equities are left alone.

    Flexible withdrawal: Each year the withdrawal is adjusted so that you take the same share of the portfolio as at the start. It only changes within the limits you chose, though, and never falls below your minimum.

    Model: Market years with more frequent extreme years, fluctuating inflation, and bonds that fall together with equities during inflation spikes, as in 2022. The stress test shows long dry spells. Ongoing costs are deducted from all returns. The returns you set apply at 2% inflation; if inflation differs, bonds and the money market follow it fully, equities by three quarters.

    Taxes: Every sale is taxed as in a real German account, including when the pots are refilled: capital gains tax plus solidarity surcharge on the gain, with partial exemption, saver's allowance and the annual advance lump sum. Fixed assumptions: 30% unrealised gain in Pot 3 at the start, €1,000 allowance, base rate 3.2%. In poor market years there is hardly any tax, in good years more. The comparison portfolio is taxed the same way, but its annual rebalancing is treated as tax-free.

    For comparison:

    Research: A study using data from 38 countries finds a safe withdrawal of about 2.3 to 3% of starting capital per year. Pots add no extra safety compared with a simple, mixed portfolio; their advantage is that you stay calm in a crisis. Flexible withdrawals make a plan considerably safer.

    Not included: state pension, church tax. A model calculation, not a forecast and not investment advice.
